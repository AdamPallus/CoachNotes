# CoachNotes UX anchor: the 30-client week

Liz coaches more than 30 people online. Every week she moves between Everfit messages, check-ins, calls, program changes, and personal follow-ups. The facts she needs are scattered across app history, notes, and paper, so the expensive part is not writing another note: it is remembering who needs attention, reconstructing why, and carrying the right context into the next interaction.

She opens CoachNotes in short gaps between client work. Mission Control should tell her which loops need closing now, which clients are missing decision-critical information, and who has gone quiet. She should be able to complete a simple follow-up there, then open a client only when she needs the fuller story.

On a client dashboard she needs a fast pre-session read: current situation, open to-dos, high-priority concerns, missing information, flags, and recent evidence. Adding a note should feel like capturing the interaction she just had. Ask should feel like choosing a useful job and writing the request, not configuring a query engine.

Everfit remains required. CoachNotes earns its place by being the coach's memory and decision layer: it preserves context, makes workload visible, supports weekly messaging and meetings, and produces clean client-facing or Everfit-ready output when needed.

## Product tests

- Can Liz identify the next client action in under ten seconds?
- Can she see missing context before a meeting or message?
- Can she close a simple loop without opening a full profile?
- Can she add a note or request a draft without navigating a form maze?
- Can she understand every number or visual without an invented legend?
- Does every persistent control help navigation, status, or action?
